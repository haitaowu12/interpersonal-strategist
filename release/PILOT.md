# Formative pilot kit

Status: protocol and templates only; no participants recruited or outcomes
collected. The pilot is a 10–20-episode usability and harm-finding exercise, not
a trial establishing general efficacy. Keep the broader product domains
experimental. The first pilot covers adults preparing ordinary workplace
conversations and everyday boundaries, with English and Chinese represented
where qualified reviewers are available. Do not broaden eligibility to fill a
quota.

## Ownership before recruitment

Assign a coordinator, an independent safety/method reviewer, fluent reviewers,
a data custodian, and a working private incident route. Record conflicts of
interest. The owner may coordinate but cannot substitute for every independent
reviewer. Obtain any institutional review or organizational authorization the
actual setting requires; this document does not determine those obligations.

Define storage, access list, deletion date, contact route, and compensation (if
any) before inviting people. Refusing or withdrawing must not affect employment,
access to support, or compensation already earned. Do not recruit direct reports
under pressure. Use an approved host; session-only by default.

## Invitation and consent text to complete

> We are testing an experimental conversation-preparation tool. It may give
> mistaken or unhelpful advice. Participation is voluntary. You may skip a
> question, stop, or decline follow-up. Do not share confidential records or
> identifiable third-party details. The tool is not a substitute for an
> authorized professional or emergency support. We will collect [minimal fields]
> in [approved storage], accessible to [roles], until [deletion date]. Contact
> [private route] to report a problem or request deletion. Host/provider data
> practices are described in [reviewed notice]. We do not promise deletion from
> systems outside our control. Do you consent to this episode and separately to
> one follow-up after your planned action or decision window?

Do not deploy this text with unfilled fields. Record consent and follow-up
permission separately. No recording, transcript retention, screenshots, or
persistent dossier is implied by participation.

## Episode flow

At entry, confirm adult eligibility, task, consent, language, and the bounded
action window. Defer cases requiring specialist crisis, abuse, safeguarding,
medical or legal determinations to an appropriate route; do not use the pilot
as an intervention in those cases.

Before use, record the participant's intended next move and confidence only if
they agree. Use a short category description rather than their story. After use,
ask whether the next move is usable, whether it preserves their voice, which
assumption changed, and whether any advice felt unsafe or pressured. Distinguish
feeling understood from improved decision quality. Do not require action.

At the agreed review event, ask what the participant chose, whether wording
needed correction, and whether there was an unintended commitment, escalation,
privacy problem, or increased certainty without evidence. Record whether they
can now proceed without further analysis. Missing follow-up is missing—not
success. A decision not to act can be useful. Do not label reconciliation or
continued engagement as universal success.

## Minimal episode record

Use `templates/pilot-episode.json` privately. Fields include pseudonymous episode
ID; subject identity; consent/eligibility; language/task category; usable-next-move
rating; initial and follow-up helpfulness; action status; assumption correction;
repeated-analysis tendency; adverse-event review status; follow-up status; and
retention deadline. Keep names, employer names, contact details, raw chats and
intimate facts out of the record. Use role IDs for reviewers.

## Stop and respond

Stop an episode on request, distress, privacy exposure, coercive advice, unsafe
recommendation, unauthorized memory action, or a host identity/capability mismatch.
Suspend the affected feature/version after a material incident pending review.
Do not ask someone to repeat a harmful interaction to obtain a better metric.
Record a minimal private incident, notify the assigned reviewer, correct or
withdraw the recommendation, and offer the appropriate support route without
inventing emergency contacts. Preserve the failure in the report. Only an
assigned reviewer may close the incident; closure is not erasure.

## Report and exit

Report recruited, consented, completed, withdrawn and follow-up counts separately.
Summarize usability, changed assumptions, action/not-action, authenticity,
adverse events, and missing data by language and task. Avoid percentages without
denominators or population efficacy claims. A 10–20-episode formative pilot
cannot establish that the tool helps everyone.

Proceed only when the existing release gates are met, no material adverse event
remains unresolved, and the independent reviewer accepts the scoped claim.
Otherwise fix the demonstrated defect or reduce the claim/scope. Do not expand
features as a substitute for evidence. The beta's success criterion is usable,
autonomy-preserving assistance—not longer sessions, retention, or dependence.
