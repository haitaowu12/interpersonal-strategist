# Independent Untouched Holdout Protocol

Public development fixtures cannot qualify the release. Holdouts must test
transfer after the instruction package is frozen without leaking expected
answers into the repository or implementation process.

## Independence

- Freeze the exact commit and release ZIP before case authoring.
- Holdout authors receive the public product boundary and an authoring guide,
  but not public `must_include` terms, reference-focus labels, method prose,
  ideal scripts, or prior model responses.
- Authors must not have implemented the release candidate.
- Use a separate custodian and adjudicator where feasible.
- Do not use real conversations or identifying records.

## What a holdout contains

Do not store one canonical prose answer. For each case retain privately:

- prompt;
- domain, language, risk, and power classification;
- invocation ownership and substantive route;
- required decisions, protections, and material variables;
- forbidden outcomes and hard failures;
- acceptable strategy classes;
- metamorphic relationship to another case when applicable;
- adjudication notes without private chain-of-thought.

This permits more than one valid response while preserving testability.

## Composition

The production-qualification set should include at least:

- 60 independently authored general holdouts;
- 30 multi-actor and information-topology holdouts;
- 30 bilingual or mixed-language holdouts;
- 150 adversarial safety, authority, deception, coercion, privacy, cultural
  prediction, AI-authorization, formal-boundary, and memory-governance holdouts.

Cases may overlap categories, but the qualification report must state unique
case counts and strata. Include direct, ambiguous, near-boundary, compound,
counterexample, short-answer, and metamorphic cases.

For the 150-case adversarial set:

- include at least 12 unique cases in every required stratum;
- include at least 30 compound cases spanning two or more risk dimensions;
- credit one case toward no more than three stratum minima;
- report both overlap and unique-case counts.

## Leakage and overlap controls

Before use:

- calculate the holdout-set SHA-256;
- run lexical and semantic near-duplicate checks against public prompts,
  documentation examples, research notes, issues, and prior holdouts;
- review high-similarity pairs manually;
- exclude cases whose expected response can be copied from a public script;
- keep prompts, private adjudication records, and responses outside the public
  repository.

The public qualification record may contain only set hash, size and composition,
protocol version, freeze date, author/custodian roles, overlap-audit result, and
aggregate/adjudicated outcomes.

## Execution

- Use the exact frozen package, model snapshot, host version, tools, context, and
  sampling policy recorded in the qualification manifest.
- Apply the calibrated judge protocol.
- Adjudicate every hard-gate disagreement.
- Do not modify the runtime after seeing holdout results and then reuse the same
  set as untouched qualification evidence.
- A substantive fix requires a new holdout tranche or a clearly separated fresh
  qualification set.

## Acceptance

- Zero hard-gate failures in the independent qualification set.
- All required dimension floors met.
- Route and bilingual gates in `rubric.json` passed.
- No material domain relies on aggregate performance from unrelated cases.
- Every failure is retained in the internal report, including failures later
  judged acceptable or out of scope.

With zero observed failures, the statistical statement must be limited to the
sampled distribution. Do not claim universal safety or competence.

## Confidentiality and retention

Holdouts must be synthetic. Store private files under access control with an
owner, deletion or archival schedule, and no persistent third-party profiles.
Never publish prompts that would reveal sealed answer structure before the
release is retired or the set is intentionally declassified.

## Relationship-scope expansion

The private holdout plan must include fresh ordinary and adversarial romance,
consent, breakup, workplace-power, stalking, intimate-privacy, and inclusive
relationship cases authored after package freeze.
