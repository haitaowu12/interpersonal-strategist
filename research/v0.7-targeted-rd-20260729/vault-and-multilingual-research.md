## Research question

Which transferable knowledge from workplace interaction, organizational
behavior, communication, negotiation, conflict, and non-English research can
make the skill more useful without turning cultural context into stereotypes
or treating individual skill as a remedy for structural harm?

## Local knowledge-base findings

The local vault contained several useful practitioner and systems-practice
donors:

- communication training: audience, purpose, active listening, clarification,
  feedback, and the left-hand/right-hand distinction between spoken dialogue
  and private assumptions;
- performance-conversation training: behavior and results, facts rather than
  judgment, listening, measurable action, timing, and practice through role
  play;
- position-versus-interest training: open, closed, probing, and clarifying
  questions plus separation of demands from underlying concerns and hopes;
- EQ training: self-awareness, emotional regulation, cognitive empathy,
  conflict participation, and feedback-seeking;
- systems-practice methods: stakeholder mapping, missing-voice protection,
  psychological safety, receiver-shaped listening, feedback-loop closure, and
  separating structural resistance from individual attitude.

The product implication is not to import these notes. It is to strengthen four
runtime mechanisms:

1. make private assumptions visible and testable;
2. distinguish comprehension from agreement;
3. convert conversations into measurable actions and review loops;
4. distinguish individual interaction skill from organizational authority,
   workload, climate, and protection.

## Multilingual and cross-context findings

### Chinese-context research

- Li and Li's 2025 meta-analysis covers 72 independent samples and 24,662
  participants. Workplace exclusion was negatively associated with positive
  work behavior and positively associated with negative work behavior in the
  included Chinese-context studies. This supports taking repeated exclusion
  seriously while preserving motive uncertainty.
- Zhang and colleagues' multilevel study of 297 employees in 42 teams found
  associations among relationship-based HR practices, psychological safety,
  and occupational well-being. It supports examining fairness and informal
  relationship channels, not using “guanxi” as a personality or national
  explanation.
- Chinese employee-voice studies reinforce that psychological safety, voice
  efficacy, exchange relationships, and power-distance orientation can be
  distinct factors. “Speak up” is therefore not a context-free instruction.

### Japanese-context research

- Eguchi and colleagues studied 198 working women aged 20–39. Among
  participants experiencing interpersonal conflict, low communication and low
  support were associated with greater psychological distress. The design is
  cross-sectional and population-specific, so it supports a support/access
  check rather than a causal script.
- Komori and Takano's worker survey reported that social skills were associated
  with lower stress at low or medium interpersonal stress, whereas high-stress
  cases required organizational-climate improvement first. This directly
  supports a structural-versus-skill classifier.
- Japanese telework research on online small talk and psychological safety
  suggests channel and virtuality matter, but small-talk prescriptions should
  remain context-specific.

### German-context research

- Schulze and colleagues combined qualitative reports and survey data from 145
  teleworkers. They identified conflict involving missing social feedback,
  unclear norms, norm violations, and digital barriers. This supports
  meta-communication about response times, channel purpose, and online
  behavior before attributing motive to digital cues.
- A German interdisciplinary healthcare communication intervention found
  evidence for psychological safety and communication as linked mechanisms,
  while not all measured communication or performance outcomes improved. This
  cautions against assuming a short training reliably changes team behavior.

### Spanish and French-language material

Searches surfaced recurring emphasis on assertiveness, emotion regulation,
support, and the distinction between conflict content and relationship
process. Much of the accessible material was educational, narrative, or
practitioner-oriented rather than strong workplace causal evidence. It was
therefore used only as a source of research questions and method vocabulary,
not as a runtime evidence claim.

## Transfer rules

Transfer:

- distinguish role, forum, audience, channel, and local norms;
- design private, written, small-group, or direct participation options;
- make implicit norms discussable;
- preserve face and dignity without weakening substantive boundaries;
- check voice safety and voice effectiveness separately;
- use feedback loops to learn how the local relationship actually works.

Do not transfer:

- national personality assumptions;
- “high-context” or “power distance” as a prediction of one person;
- compulsory indirectness or directness;
- relationship-based access as proof of corruption or favoritism;
- social-skills training as a remedy for retaliation, overload, or unsafe
  organizational climate.

## Implementation decisions

Applied in `0.7.0-alpha.1`:

- situation and mechanism classification;
- signal-strength and update rules;
- voice safety versus voice effectiveness;
- multi-actor decision and missing-voice mapping;
- structural-versus-individual boundary;
- digital norm mismatch as a testable class;
- culturally adaptive channel choices without national inference;
- evidence-ledger entries with population and design limitations.

Deferred until the long-running Pro review and additional behavioral work:

- full scene-playbook library;
- reciprocity and relationship-investment module;
- richer cross-language worked examples;
- exercises beyond the initial calibration, conflict, repair, negotiation, and
  commitment drills;
- independently authored holdouts and native-speaker review.
