# Interpersonal Strategist v0.7 Targeted R&D

Date: 2026-07-29

Status: implemented through `0.7.0-alpha.2`

Inputs: current `0.6.0-alpha.1`, clean-room pattern audit of
`goutoujunshi`, ChatGPT Pro research review, and independent source checks

Additional implementation evidence:
[vault and multilingual research pass](vault-and-multilingual-research.md).

The completed long-running Pro review is retained under
[`external-feedback/`](../../external-feedback/), together with the local
apply/consider/reject disposition. Its accepted recommendations were compiled
into method contracts, digital-pragmatics guidance, five additional
playbooks, ten development cases, and a selectively verified source ledger.

## Executive verdict

The current alpha is a sound **interpersonal decision-and-wording kernel**. It
can credibly help a user separate evidence from interpretation, map power and
exposure, choose a reversible first move, draft a compact message, and define
response branches and stopping conditions.

It is not yet a deep interpersonal strategist. The skill has a disciplined
process, but little mechanism knowledge behind that process. It often knows
*how to structure an answer* without enough knowledge to distinguish *which
interpersonal mechanism is driving the case*. This is most visible in conflict
diagnosis, negotiation, trust repair, feedback and accountability,
relationship-investment calibration, multi-actor dynamics, and non-workplace
scenes.

The v0.7 objective should therefore be:

> Move from a process-and-template skill to a mechanism-driven strategist,
> while remaining explicit-only, stateless, chat-only, tool-free, portable,
> and non-manipulative.

## Current boundary

| Area | Current credible boundary | Do not claim yet |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | Separate records, observations, reports, interpretations, unknowns, contradictions, and stale facts | Infer motives or offline events from sparse messages |
| Decision support | Recommend one proportionate, reversible next move and say what would change it | Diagnose the root cause of complex recurring patterns |
| Power | Map authority, dependencies, exposure, decision rights, witnesses, and alternatives | Predict retaliation or institutional outcomes |
| Communication | Draft short requests, refusals, corrections, feedback, repair, and follow-up branches | Universal scripts or guaranteed persuasion |
| Workplace | Useful first-move support for workload, priority, credit, exclusion, managing up, and boundaries | Deep organizational-politics, mediation, investigation, or labor-relations expertise |
| Everyday relationships | General non-romantic support for friends, family, roommates, neighbors, and networks | Equal depth across these scenes |
| Bilingual work | Preserve facts, agency, decision rights, boundaries, and stopping conditions in English and Simplified Chinese | Native-level social calibration across regions, industries, generations, and mixed-language teams |
| Safety and formal routes | Organize facts and prepare questions or messages for a qualified route | Legal, HR, medical, clinical, safeguarding, or crisis determinations |

### Hard scope boundary to retain

- No romance, dating, intimacy, breakup, reconciliation, or sexual-consent
  strategy. A dedicated relationship-support skill already owns that lane.
- No psychological diagnosis, personality typing, manipulability scoring, or
  hidden social scoring.
- No legal or formal HR classification.
- No coercion, deception, retaliation, humiliation, covert surveillance,
  rumor engineering, or pressure after refusal.
- No connectors, automatic sending, persistent profiles, or cross-task memory.
- No claim that a cited association establishes a specific person's motive or
  a guaranteed intervention outcome.

## What to learn from `goutoujunshi`

The donor skill's advantage is not simply “more words.” Its useful product
pattern is layered:

1. a compact behavioral kernel;
2. progressive-disclosure knowledge modules;
3. practical scene guides;
4. strategy selection and tone calibration;
5. response branches, observation windows, and stop rules;
6. exercises and scenario tests;
7. a source index with evidence caveats.

These patterns can be adopted clean-room.

Do not import its distinctive language, examples, questionnaires, or corpus.
Do not adopt romance progression, physical-contact guidance, mandatory MBTI,
0–100 relationship scoring, gender heuristics, persistent target profiles, or
“creator-style” attraction tactics. The installed donor is PolyForm
Noncommercial 1.0.0, while this project is MIT licensed.

### Layer 2: twelve scene playbooks

1. managing-up disagreement;
2. workload and incompatible priorities;
3. feedback to a peer or direct report;
4. credit and attribution correction;
5. exclusion from access or a decision forum;
6. sponsorship, networking, and asking for advocacy;
7. conflict de-escalation and repair;
8. refusal and recurring-boundary enforcement;
9. asking for help, access, or a favor;
10. difficult friend, family, roommate, or neighbor interaction;
11. formal-route preparation without formal classification;
12. disengagement, exposure reduction, or exit.

Each playbook should route to one or two methods, not restate the entire skill.
It should include a scene classifier, low/medium/high-exposure options,
copy-ready wording, positive/ambiguous/negative/no-response branches, an
observation window, escalation trigger, and stopping condition.

### Layer 3: evidence and evaluation

- `sources/claim-ledger.md`: claim, source, source type, population/context,
  limitation, review date, and permissible runtime wording.
- `examples/calibration-cases.md`: synthetic worked examples and
  counterexamples.
- development evals: at least 60 cases, including 18 independently reviewed
  English–Chinese functional mirrors.
- holdouts: at least 20 independently authored cases kept outside the public
  development fixtures.

The runtime should normally load the kernel, one method module, and at most one
playbook. It should not load the whole library.

## Core method contracts

### 1. Evidence–Interpretation–Test

- **Use:** ambiguous behavior or alleged motive.
- **Inputs:** record, source, timing, channel, impact, and decision.
- **Method:** separate observation from interpretation; name the leading
  explanation and strongest evidence-linked alternative; identify a
  discriminating observation; choose a low-cost test useful under both.
- **Output:** evidence boundary plus testable next move.
- **Failure modes:** inventing alternatives, endless testing, excusing a
  documented boundary breach, or demanding certainty before a safe action.
- **Evidence:** attribution and meta-perception research supports humility, not
  a universal bias-correction script.
- **Example:** A manager has not answered a priority question. Ask for a
  choice between two delivery consequences before inferring avoidance.

### 2. Power–Dependency–Voice–Fair Process

- **Use:** asymmetric authority, retaliation exposure, or contested decisions.
- **Inputs:** decision rights, dependencies, alternatives, audience, forum,
  policy route, and exposure.
- **Method:** map who decides, who supplies critical resources or information,
  whose voice can enter which forum, and whether the process allows input,
  explanation, consistency, and correction; choose direct, buffered, supported,
  or formal-route preparation.
- **Output:** calibrated channel, audience, wording, and protection step.
- **Failure modes:** treating psychological safety as an individual confidence
  problem; assuming a polite invitation makes a process safe; declaring a
  process legally unfair.
- **Evidence:** psychological safety and justice are associated with voice and
  work outcomes, but intervention evidence is context-dependent.
- **Example:** A junior employee raises a safety concern privately with the
  decision owner, preserves the technical record, and identifies the next
  qualified route if the issue is not addressed.

### 3. Conflict Type–Heat–Repair

- **Use:** disagreement, escalation, or relationship damage.
- **Inputs:** issue type, pattern, audience, arousal, prior attempts, harm, and
  ongoing dependency.
- **Method:** distinguish task, process/resource, relationship/status, values,
  and safety/formal-route conflict; assess heat and reversibility; choose
  clarify, structure, pause, mediate, repair, document, or disengage.
- **Output:** conflict classification, immediate move, and re-entry condition.
- **Failure modes:** assuming task conflict is beneficial, pushing joint
  dialogue in coercive or unsafe cases, or treating a pause as silent
  punishment.
- **Evidence:** relationship conflict is consistently harmful; task conflict
  is not reliably beneficial and depends on context.
- **Example:** Two peers disagree about the design and begin attacking
  competence. Pause the meeting, separate decision criteria from personal
  claims, and reconvene with an owner and decision rule.

### 4. Trust Repair

- **Use:** a concrete miss, breach, or credibility loss.
- **Inputs:** verified behavior, impact, responsibility, repairability,
  recurrence, and requested remedy.
- **Method:** acknowledge facts and impact; take responsibility that is
  supported; explain without displacing responsibility; repair the loss;
  change the process; create observable proof over time.
- **Output:** repair message plus behavior-based verification window.
- **Failure modes:** using apology as a tactic, confessing unsupported intent,
  demanding forgiveness, or promising trust restoration.
- **Evidence:** experimental work suggests violation type and response matter;
  it does not yield a universal apology-versus-denial rule.
- **Example:** After missing a handoff, name the delay, restore the deliverable,
  add an earlier warning checkpoint, and let future reliability—not the
  apology—carry the proof.

### 5. Interests–Alternatives–Criteria–Commitments

- **Use:** incompatible requests, resources, timelines, or preferences.
- **Inputs:** each side's stated position, underlying interests and
  constraints, alternatives, authority, standards, and implementation risks.
- **Method:** separate positions from interests; define the user's credible
  alternative; generate more than one feasible option; use legitimate
  criteria; specify owner, action, date, dependency, warning point, and review.
- **Output:** option comparison and implementable agreement.
- **Failure modes:** revealing sensitive limits, inventing the other side's
  interests, using “win-win” to pressure agreement, or accepting an agreement
  worse than a credible alternative.
- **Evidence:** principled negotiation is a useful practitioner framework;
  information sharing and cooperative orientation have empirical support, but
  the branded framework is not validated as one unified intervention.
- **Example:** Replace “I can't do both” with two delivery packages showing
  what each preserves and displaces, then ask the decision owner to choose.

### 6. Behavior–Impact–Expectation–Commitment

- **Use:** feedback, accountability, or repeat performance problems.
- **Inputs:** specific behavior, impact, controllability, required standard,
  context, support, authority, and review point.
- **Method:** confirm readiness and forum; describe behavior; state concrete
  impact; invite material context; define the standard; agree on support,
  action, warning trigger, and review.
- **Output:** bounded conversation plan and follow-up record.
- **Failure modes:** identity threat, vague praise/criticism, feedback on
  uncontrollable outcomes, public correction, or excessive feedback.
- **Evidence:** feedback has heterogeneous effects and sometimes reduces
  performance; task focus and actionability matter.
- **Example:** Discuss two late handoffs and their scheduling effect, agree on
  a one-day warning trigger, and review after the next two handoffs.

### 7. Boundary–Enforcement–Escalation–Stop

- **Use:** recurring requests, overload, disrespect, unwanted contact, or
  failed direct attempts.
- **Inputs:** behavior, user-controlled limit, feasibility, power, exposure,
  prior attempts, and current qualified routes.
- **Method:** state what the user will or will not do; offer a feasible
  alternative only if desired; name the user-controlled next action; define
  documentation, escalation, and stop conditions.
- **Output:** enforceable boundary with contingency.
- **Failure modes:** threats the user cannot carry out, disguised punishment,
  pseudo-legal language, repeated pursuit, or unsafe confrontation.
- **Evidence:** official and practitioner guidance supports clarity,
  documentation, and route awareness; universal outcome claims are not
  justified.
- **Example:** “I can cover this shift today. I cannot be the default backup
  next week. Please assign an owner by Friday; otherwise I will remain on my
  scheduled shifts.”

### 8. Reciprocity–Reliability–Investment

- **Use:** uncertain whether a work or everyday relationship is temporarily
  uneven or chronically one-way.
- **Inputs:** 3–5 meaningful recent events, initiation, follow-through,
  responsiveness to stated needs, repair, burden, and alternatives.
- **Method:** review events rather than message counts; distinguish temporary
  constraint, different contribution form, negotiable mismatch, chronic
  non-repair, and explicit refusal; clarify once when warranted; observe a
  natural event; recalibrate or exit without punishment.
- **Output:** maintain, renegotiate, reduce investment, or disengage.
- **Failure modes:** transactional scorekeeping, universal ratios, testing
  loyalty, or treating all asymmetry as exploitation.
- **Evidence:** social-exchange theory is influential but broad and
  under-specified; use it as a decision scaffold, not a law.
- **Example:** A friend declines two plans but proposes alternatives and
  follows through; classify this as schedule mismatch, not one-way investment.

### 9. Stakeholder Interface Map

- **Use:** more than two actors or a hidden decision forum.
- **Inputs:** actors, roles, authority, information, dependencies, affected
  parties, current forum, and legitimate routes.
- **Method:** map interfaces and decision flow; identify verifier, mediator,
  sponsor, protector, and decision owner; choose the smallest legitimate
  coalition needed for the objective.
- **Output:** contact sequence and forum strategy.
- **Failure modes:** secret influence scores, moral ranking by power,
  manufactured alliances, gossip, or static maps.
- **Evidence:** stakeholder salience is a conceptual heuristic, not a
  validated interpersonal scoring system.
- **Example:** For repeated scope changes, involve the project owner and
  scheduling owner in one priority decision rather than lobbying unrelated
  colleagues.

### 10. Channel and Bilingual Function Adapter

- **Use:** consequential written/synchronous or English–Chinese communication.
- **Inputs:** purpose, ambiguity, record need, roles, audience, urgency, shared
  proficiency, and observed local norms.
- **Method:** choose channel by information and record needs; preserve facts,
  agency, decision right, request, boundary, consequence, and stop rule; vary
  buffering and sentence order without changing force; run a parity check.
- **Output:** channel plan and functionally equivalent drafts.
- **Failure modes:** national stereotypes, literal translation, status
  inflation, weakened Chinese asks, or more accusatory English.
- **Evidence:** mediated communication can distort tone; cross-cultural effects
  are heterogeneous. Consequential bilingual drafts still require fluent,
  context-aware human review.
- **Example:** Both drafts ask the owner to choose A-by-Thursday or B-by-Monday;
  the Chinese version may add context but cannot hide the trade-off.

### Reject

- mandatory intake questionnaire;
- MBTI or other psychometric typing;
- relationship, status, attractiveness, or influence scores;
- hidden coalition rankings;
- persistent profiles;
- universal tone rules by nationality or gender;
- scripts designed to manufacture fear, dependence, jealousy, or urgency;
- copying or closely paraphrasing donor content.

### Needs user decision before implementation freeze

1. Keep the product **workplace-first with selective everyday coverage**
   (recommended), or pursue equal depth across family/friend/roommate/community
   cases in v0.7.
2. Keep bilingual support at **functional parity with mandatory human-review
   caveats** (recommended), or fund deeper native-language playbook review
   before v0.7.
3. Keep citations internal by default (recommended), or expose compact source
   notes in ordinary user answers.

## Acceptance criteria for a v0.7 implementation

- At least 60 development cases and 20 independently authored holdouts.
- At least 18 independently reviewed English–Chinese functional mirrors.
- Every consequential recommendation identifies the active mechanism,
  evidence boundary, preferred move, observation window, change trigger, and
  stop condition.
- At least 90% correct routing on unambiguous mechanism cases.
- At least 85% of ambiguous cases contain two evidence-linked interpretations
  without false symmetry.
- At least 90% of power-asymmetric cases choose a channel and exposure level
  consistent with the case constraints.
- 100% refusal on coercion, retaliation, surveillance, impersonation, and
  pressure-after-refusal cases while preserving a legitimate alternative.
- 100% routing of romance/intimacy and legal/HR/medical/crisis determinations
  outside the skill.
- No source claim without a claim-ledger entry and limitation.
- No bilingual pair with changed decision right, ability to decline,
  substantive request, consequence, escalation trigger, or stop condition.
- No behavior-readiness claim until human review and untouched holdouts pass.

## Recommended implementation slice

Build v0.7 in three bounded stages:

1. **Mechanism foundation:** classifier, evidence ledger, and modules 1–4.
2. **Action foundation:** modules 5–8 and the 12 playbooks.
3. **Behavior proof:** expanded evals, bilingual review, untouched holdouts,
   packaging, and a controlled pilot.

Do not change the current installed alpha until stage 1 has its own passing
tests and review packet.
