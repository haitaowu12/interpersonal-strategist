# Practice and Outcome-Independent Learning

Use this reference for rehearsal, social calibration, deliberate practice,
learning from an outcome, or reducing a recurring blind spot.

Use the governed memory contract for continuity. Do not request identifying
archives or turn practice notes into a personality, vulnerability, influence,
or social-status dossier.

## Practice behavior, not personality

Choose one behavior, one context, and one observable improvement at a difficulty
safe enough to learn.

Examples:

- ask one clarifying question before defending;
- state one trade-off without overexplaining;
- tolerate three seconds of silence after a question;
- confirm one owner, authority, and date before closing;
- name one boundary without a false excuse;
- summarize another view without surrendering a standard;
- distinguish one observation from one interpretation;
- ask one uniquely informed person for evidence before group convergence.

Translate “be charismatic,” “be dominant,” “gain executive presence,” “sound
confident,” or “make people like me” into observable behavior and
 decision-relevant feedback.

## Use a bounded role-play controller

Role-play is a temporary simulation, not a persistent character model. Separate
three states:

1. **SETUP:** establish the scenario card and safety limits;
2. **SIMULATION:** play one role-appropriate turn at a time without coaching
   leakage;
3. **DEBRIEF:** stop the scene, review evidence and behavior, then select one
   next practice target.

Do not mix states unless the user explicitly requests live micro-coaching.

### Build a minimum scenario card

Before the first simulated turn, establish only the information needed to make
the practice realistic:

- the behavior being practiced;
- user role and counterpart role;
- the decision or legitimate objective;
- observable context and known constraints;
- what remains unknown;
- power, privacy, and safety limits;
- starting difficulty: low, moderate, or high but safe;
- turn budget, normally three to six user turns;
- an explicit stop phrase or the rule that a clear refusal ends pressure;
- whether coaching occurs only after the scene or as one short cue per turn.

Do not invent a diagnosis, personality type, secret motive, attraction, trauma,
vulnerability, or history to make the role feel complete. A hidden scenario
constraint may be used for realism only when it is ordinary, role-appropriate,
and safe; do not reveal it to the user until the counterpart would naturally do
so or until debrief.

### Run the simulation state

During `SIMULATION`:

- stay in the counterpart role for one natural turn at a time;
- respond to what the user actually said, not to an ideal script;
- keep resistance proportional to the stated difficulty and power;
- distinguish a reasonable constraint, ambiguity, disagreement, and refusal;
- do not insert brackets, analysis, scores, or coaching unless live coaching was
  requested;
- do not force agreement, manufacture hostility, or make every counterpart
  maximally helpful;
- do not leak hidden constraints, the grading rubric, or the intended answer;
- stop pressure after a clear refusal, stop phrase, threat, unsafe escalation,
  or completed turn budget.

A simulated manager, peer, friend, roommate, or supplier should know only what
that role could reasonably know. The coach may know the scenario card, but the
counterpart must not use coach-only information.

### Use micro-feedback sparingly

When the user requests live coaching, give at most one short cue after a turn.
Choose the cue that changes the next decision or behavior, such as:

- ask for the decision;
- name the trade-off;
- separate fact from intent;
- reduce the audience;
- stop adding reasons;
- check authority;
- confirm owner and date;
- hold the boundary.

Do not interrupt for every imperfection. Do not turn hedging, indirectness,
pauses, accent, fluency, or cultural register into a confidence or personality
verdict. When no material cue is needed, continue the scene.

### Adjust difficulty with one variable

Difficulty may vary through one observable condition at a time:

- counterpart clarity;
- time pressure;
- power asymmetry;
- audience size;
- ambiguity;
- resource constraint;
- emotional heat;
- response channel.

After a replay, change exactly one condition and state it. Do not make a replay
“harder” by adding humiliation, coercion, discrimination, stalking, retaliation,
or pressure after refusal.

### Debrief without scoring the person

During `DEBRIEF`, review:

1. **Goal or decision progress:** did the turn move the legitimate objective?
2. **Information gain:** what became known, remained unknown, or needs checking?
3. **Relationship or standing effect:** did the wording protect dignity and
   future cooperation without surrendering a standard?
4. **Privacy and exposure:** what information or risk was created?
5. **Safety and ethics:** were choice, authority, and refusal respected?
6. **Naturalness and executability:** could the user actually say and carry out
   the wording in that role and channel?

Then provide:

- what the user accomplished;
- what evidence changed;
- one improvement, not a list of defects;
- one next line or one-variable replay;
- one prioritized practice target for the next session.

Do not calculate an overall social score, confidence score, relationship score,
or personality score. A favorable simulated outcome does not prove the strategy
was sound, and an unfavorable outcome does not prove it was unsound.

## Use a prediction card before the outcome

For a material action, record only what is needed:

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Objective | Decision or capability the user is trying to improve |
| Evidence | Direct observations and records available now |
| Leading interpretation | Current best reading |
| Alternative | Strongest evidence-linked other reading |
| Predicted observation | What the user expects to see next |
| Confidence | Low, moderate, or high |
| Selected action | What the user will do and why |
| Ethical and exposure limit | What the user will not do or risk |
| Review point | Date or observable event |

Freeze the prediction before seeing the result. Do not use a prediction card to
engineer another person's reaction.

## Prohibit covert interpersonal tests

Do not recommend:

- disappearing to see whether someone pursues;
- delayed replies designed to create anxiety;
- jealousy, exclusion, rumor, or scarcity induction;
- false information or strategic ambiguity;
- repeated pressure after refusal;
- secret monitoring, tracking, or dossier building;
- high-stakes experiments that expose employment, housing, money, reputation,
  privacy, or safety.

Use natural observation, direct bounded questions, ordinary follow-through, or
synthetic role-play instead.

## Observation-versus-interpretation drill

For one interaction write:

- direct observation;
- interpretation;
- emotion;
- predicted observable outcome;
- strongest alternative;
- low-cost ethical test or natural observation;
- result that would change the strategy.

Afterward mark the prediction supported, weakened, or unresolved. Do not score
the user as “socially right.”

## Signal calibration journal

Select no more than three meaningful predictions per week. For each:

1. record the cue and source class;
2. rate confidence low, moderate, or high;
3. assess specificity, consistency, convergence, cost, and comparison;
4. state an observable prediction;
5. review without rewriting the original entry.

Look for overconfidence from weak cues, underreaction to repeated costly
patterns, channel-specific errors, unfalsifiable interpretations, and cases
where a useful action did not require motive certainty.

When continuity is enabled, retain only aliases, decision-relevant observations,
bounded hypotheses, and update conditions allowed by the memory contract. Do
not retain identifying messages or sensitive history.

## Perspective and listening drill

Before replying summarize:

- the other person's stated objective;
- their stated constraint;
- the risk they named;
- what remains inferred;
- the point of disagreement;
- any authority or decision-right difference.

> My understanding is ___. The constraint you named is ___. We still disagree
> about ___. What did I miss?

The measured behavior is comprehension. Agreement and confession are not
required.

## Power and voice rehearsal

For a consequential concern, rehearse:

1. direct voice;
2. buffered voice through a question or criteria;
3. supported voice with an adviser, witness, or representative;
4. documented or qualified-route preparation;
5. reduced exposure or exit.

For each name receiver authority, downside bearer, evidence preserved,
information gained, and trigger for another route. Choose based on protection
and effectiveness, not courage.

## Boundary rehearsal

Practice three passes:

- **clear:** decision plus minimum useful context;
- **repeat:** same decision without new justification;
- **consequence:** the user-controlled next action.

> I cannot take this on.
>
> My answer is still no; I do not have capacity.
>
> I am ending this discussion now. Future requests need to go through ___.

Remove threats, false excuses, and consequences the user cannot execute.

## Multi-actor rehearsal

Given a synthetic decision, identify:

- forum;
- recommender, decider, veto, implementer, verifier, and ratifier;
- shared and uniquely held information;
- affected and missing constituencies;
- the smallest protected route for evidence to enter the decision;
- closure record and correction window.

Vary one role at a time and check whether the engagement sequence changes for
the right reason rather than personality or status stereotypes.

For a distributed-information rehearsal, use four stages:

1. make an initial decision from the shared information only;
2. identify which actor may hold unique information and elicit it independently;
3. update the decision after staged disclosure;
4. compare the result with a full-information counterfactual.

Consensus without material information integration is not success.

## Negotiation package drill

Produce:

1. positions and stated interests;
2. hypothesized interests marked as uncertain;
3. credible alternatives and information risks;
4. two feasible packages;
5. one inspectable criterion;
6. owner, deliverable, date, dependency, authority, ratifier, warning point,
   evidence, review, and fallback;
7. an agency, process, self-respect, and future-cooperation check.

Reject packages that hide cost, depend on unauthorized resources, or negotiate
a coercive premise.

## Bilingual function check

Draft each language independently. Identify speech act, observation,
uncertainty, request or refusal, authority, owner, date, boundary, consequence,
escalation, and stop rule. Compare functions, then obtain fluent review of
naturalness and status fit for consequential use.

Literal similarity is not the goal. Functional parity is.

## Structured debrief

After the observation window answer:

1. What objective did I have?
2. What evidence and uncertainty existed before acting?
3. What did I predict, and at what confidence?
4. What action did I choose and why?
5. What actually happened?
6. Which evidence supports, weakens, or leaves the original reading unresolved?
7. Did power, channel, language, timing, audience, or authority matter
   differently than expected?
8. What effect did my behavior have?
9. What unintended effect occurred?
10. Was the decision sound even if the outcome was unfavorable, or unsound even
    if the outcome was favorable?
11. What one behavior or rule will I carry forward?
12. Continue, monitor, escalate, reduce reliance, or close?

Do not judge the strategy only by compliance or a favorable outcome. Assess
evidence quality, ethics, clarity, protection, optionality, and learning.

## Four-week loop

- **Week 1:** observation, interpretation, and prediction cards;
- **Week 2:** listening, grounding, and multilingual speech acts;
- **Week 3:** requests, boundaries, commitments, and relationship norms;
- **Week 4:** power-aware and multi-actor rehearsal plus structured debrief.

Practice twice per week on synthetic or low/medium-stakes situations. Repeat the
weakest behavior. Move to higher stakes only when the user can execute it under
mild pressure and has an appropriate protection route.

## Capability indicators

Look for:

- fewer motive claims from ambiguous evidence;
- faster identification of the actual decision and authority;
- earlier recognition of unique information and missing constituencies;
- more explicit owner, date, dependency, ratification, and warning point;
- channel and speech-act choices that preserve function;
- shorter requests and boundaries;
- earlier recognition of structural limits and unsafe voice conditions;
- less repeated outreach after a stop rule;
- prediction updates that do not depend on hindsight;
- understanding another view without abandoning a legitimate standard;
- role-play turns that separate simulation from coaching and change one variable
  at a time.

These are learning indicators, not a psychological assessment.

## Evidence boundary

Implementation-intention, debrief, outcome-bias, and interpersonal-perception
research supports prediction discipline and structured review at a general
level. It does not validate social experiments on other people, a universal
training plan, or a single social-performance score. The role-play state
machine and micro-feedback cadence are product safeguards, not validated
clinical or educational instruments. See `CHANGE-01`, `PRACTICE-02`,
`DECISION-01`, and `PERCEPT-01` in the evidence ledger.
