# Practice and After-Action Learning

Use this reference when the user wants to build interpersonal skill over time,
rehearse a conversation, improve social calibration, learn from an outcome, or
reduce a repeated blind spot.

The skill remains stateless. The user may keep their own private notes, but the
assistant must not create a persistent profile or request identifying
conversation archives.

## Use deliberate practice, not personality scoring

Choose one behavior, one context, and one observable improvement. Practice at a
difficulty that is challenging but safe enough to learn.

Examples:

- ask one clarifying question before defending;
- state one trade-off without overexplaining;
- tolerate three seconds of silence after a question;
- confirm one owner and date before ending a meeting;
- name one boundary without a false excuse;
- summarize the other view before disagreeing;
- distinguish one observation from one interpretation.

Do not set goals such as “be charismatic,” “be more dominant,” or “make people
like me.” Translate them into observable behavior and feedback.

## 1. Observation versus interpretation drill

For one interaction, write:

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Direct observation | What was said or done |
| Interpretation | What meaning I assigned |
| Emotion | What I felt |
| Prediction | What I expect next |
| Alternative | Strongest evidence-linked other reading |
| Test | Low-cost action or observation |
| Update | What result would change my view |

Review after the result. Score only whether the prediction was supported,
weakened, or unresolved—not whether the user was “socially right.”

## 2. Signal calibration journal

Select no more than three meaningful predictions per week. For each:

1. write the cue;
2. rate confidence as low, moderate, or high;
3. record specificity, consistency, convergence, cost, and comparison;
4. state the predicted observable outcome;
5. review without rewriting the original prediction.

Look for:

- overconfidence from low-specificity cues;
- underreaction to repeated costly patterns;
- different accuracy by channel or relationship;
- interpretations that cannot be falsified;
- situations where action was useful despite unresolved motive.

Do not record sensitive details or build dossiers about other people.

## 3. Perspective and listening drill

Before replying, summarize:

- the other person's stated objective;
- their stated constraint;
- the risk they named;
- what remains inferred;
- the point of disagreement.

Use:

> My understanding is ___. The main constraint you named is ___. We still
> disagree about ___. What did I miss?

Ask the other person to correct the summary. Comprehension is the measured
behavior; agreement is not required.

## 4. Left-Hand/Right-Hand conversation review

Create two columns:

- what was said;
- what the user thought, predicted, or withheld.

Classify each private item as fact, interpretation, emotion, need, boundary,
prediction, or request. Decide whether to:

- ask;
- state;
- test;
- keep private;
- route elsewhere.

The exercise builds choice about disclosure. It does not prescribe total
transparency.

## 5. Power and voice rehearsal

For a consequential concern, rehearse four routes:

1. direct;
2. buffered through a question or criteria;
3. supported by a witness, adviser, or representative;
4. documented or formal-route preparation.

For each route, name:

- receiver authority;
- interpersonal and material exposure;
- evidence preserved;
- likely information gained;
- trigger for moving to the next route.

Choose based on safety and effectiveness, not courage.

## 6. Boundary rehearsal

Practice three passes:

- **clear:** decision plus brief context;
- **repeat:** same decision without new justification;
- **consequence:** the user-controlled next action.

Example:

> I cannot take this on.
>
> My answer is still no; I do not have capacity.
>
> I am ending this discussion now. Future requests need to go through ___.

Remove threats, false excuses, and consequences the user cannot execute.

## 7. Negotiation package drill

Take one apparent yes/no dispute and produce:

1. the user's position and interests;
2. the other side's stated position and hypothesized interests;
3. the user's credible alternative;
4. two packages that vary scope, time, owner, resource, or assurance;
5. one legitimate criterion;
6. the full Commitment Reliability Check.

Reject any package that hides material cost or depends on unauthorized
resources.

## 8. Role-play with branches

Ask the assistant to play the other role realistically, not maximally hostile
or agreeable.

Run three branches:

- reasonable constraint;
- ambiguous response;
- refusal or defensiveness.

After each turn, coach only:

- what the user accomplished;
- what evidence changed;
- one improvement;
- the next line.

Do not role-play coercion, humiliation, stalking, retaliation, or pressure
after refusal.

## 9. Bilingual function check

For consequential English-Chinese communication:

1. draft the message independently in each language;
2. identify observation, uncertainty, request, owner, date, boundary,
   consequence, escalation, and stop rule;
3. compare each function;
4. ask a fluent reviewer to check naturalness and relationship calibration.

Literal similarity is not the goal. Functional parity is.

## 10. After-action review

After the observation window, answer:

1. What objective did I have?
2. What did I observe before acting?
3. What interpretation and confidence did I use?
4. What action did I choose, and why?
5. What actually happened?
6. What supports or weakens the original reading?
7. Did power, channel, timing, or audience matter differently than expected?
8. What effect did my own behavior have?
9. What remains unknown?
10. What one rule or behavior will I carry into the next similar case?

Do not judge strategy only by whether the other person complied. Also assess
evidence quality, ethics, clarity, protection of options, and learning.

## 11. Spaced practice plan

Use a four-week loop:

- **Week 1:** observation versus interpretation;
- **Week 2:** listening and clarification;
- **Week 3:** requests, boundaries, and commitments;
- **Week 4:** power-aware rehearsal and after-action review.

Practice twice per week on low- or medium-stakes synthetic or real situations.
Repeat the weakest skill for another cycle. Move to higher-stakes use only
after the user can execute the behavior under mild pressure.

## Skill-growth indicators

Look for:

- fewer motive claims from ambiguous evidence;
- faster identification of the actual decision;
- more explicit owner, date, dependency, and warning point;
- better channel choice;
- shorter, clearer requests and boundaries;
- earlier recognition of structural or authority limits;
- less repeated outreach after a stop rule;
- more accurate prediction updates;
- ability to understand another view without abandoning a legitimate standard.

These are learning indicators, not a psychological assessment.
