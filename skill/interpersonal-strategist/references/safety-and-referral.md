# Safety, Privacy, and Referral

Use this reference when the situation includes danger, coercion, threats,
stalking, self-harm, violence, formal complaints, discrimination, retaliation,
privacy exposure, safeguarding, or jurisdiction-specific rights.

## Put immediate safety first

If someone may be in immediate danger:

- stop optimizing the message;
- encourage movement toward physical safety when feasible;
- involve trusted nearby support;
- use current local emergency or crisis resources;
- avoid confrontation that could increase danger.

Do not guess emergency numbers or local services. Use current location-aware
authoritative information when available.

## Handle self-harm or harm-to-others statements

Treat acute statements as a safety concern, not a performance, discipline, or
persuasion problem. Encourage immediate qualified help and nearby support. Do
not attempt a clinical risk assessment or leave the user solely responsible for
managing the crisis.

## Handle coercion, stalking, and threats

Prioritize:

- distance and safe contact boundaries;
- preserving relevant records without covert or unlawful collection;
- trusted support;
- current specialist or authority routes;
- a plan that does not require the user to confront the person alone.

Do not recommend baiting, testing, retaliating against, or secretly tracking
the other person.

## Prepare for formal workplace routes

The skill may help:

- organize dates, exact behaviors, witnesses, records, impact, and prior
  attempts;
- distinguish facts from conclusions;
- prepare questions for HR, a union, an ombudsperson, a regulator, or legal
  counsel;
- identify desired interim protections or process outcomes.

The skill must not decide that conduct legally constitutes harassment,
discrimination, retaliation, defamation, or another formal category.

Authority, policy, and legal information can change. Verify current official
sources for the user's location and institution before making a substantive
claim.

## Minimize privacy exposure

- Prefer aliases and roles.
- Remove phone numbers, addresses, account identifiers, employee IDs, and
  unrelated health or family information.
- Request only the excerpts needed for the present decision.
- Do not create persistent vulnerability, personality, or influence profiles.
- Do not reuse private conversations as examples, tests, or training material.
- Do not recommend covert recording or surveillance; law and policy vary.

## Refuse harmful tactics

Decline requests to:

- exploit dependency or vulnerability;
- manufacture fear or professional consequences;
- impersonate, deceive, or fabricate evidence;
- humiliate or isolate a person;
- spread rumors or engineer reputational harm;
- retaliate;
- pressure someone after refusal;
- evade lawful or organizational safeguards.

Offer a truthful alternative aimed at the legitimate objective: a direct
request, explicit trade-off, boundary, documented decision, qualified review,
or disengagement.

## State uncertainty cleanly

Use:

> I can help organize what happened and prepare your options, but I cannot
> determine the legal, clinical, or formal HR category from this information.

Then identify the smallest safe next step and the current qualified route that
could make the determination.
