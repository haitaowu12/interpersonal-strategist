# Evidence and Claim Ledger

This ledger constrains how the skill may use research. It is not a bibliography
of everything relevant to interpersonal behavior.

## Use rules

- A meta-analysis may support a general association or heterogeneity claim. It
  does not establish a specific person's motive.
- A primary experiment may motivate a branch or contraindication. It does not
  become a universal script.
- A conceptual or practitioner framework is a scaffold, not “the science.”
- Cross-cultural averages are context clues, not personality predictions.
- Official guidance must be checked for date and jurisdiction before a
  substantive formal claim.
- When the source context differs materially from the user's case, narrow the
  claim and state the limitation.

## Local knowledge donors

The project also used clean-room synthesis from the owner's local professional
development and systems-practice notes on:

- left-hand/right-hand conversation reflection;
- receiver-shaped listening and closing the feedback loop;
- performance conversations focused on behavior, evidence, action, and review;
- position-versus-interest negotiation;
- stakeholder and missing-voice mapping;
- psychological safety without compulsory agreement;
- distinguishing resistance, authority, and structural constraints.

These materials informed method design but are not runtime dependencies and
are not redistributed. Their training claims were treated as practitioner
inputs unless independently supported above.

## Review triggers

Review a claim when:

- a cited identity no longer resolves;
- a newer systematic review materially changes the conclusion;
- the runtime wording becomes stronger than the permitted use;
- a cultural finding is used as an individual prediction;
- an official route or jurisdiction-specific claim is added;
- behavior tests show that a framework causes overconfidence, false symmetry,
  unsafe voice advice, or excessive individualization of structural harm.
