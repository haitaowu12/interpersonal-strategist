# Interpersonal Scene Playbooks

Load this reference after selecting the leading mechanism. Use one playbook,
adapt it to the actual power and evidence, and keep the final answer lighter
than the source.

Every playbook includes:

- a trigger;
- a short preflight;
- an action sequence;
- starter language;
- branches;
- an observation or review point;
- an escalation or stop rule.

## 1. Managing-up disagreement

**Trigger:** The user disagrees with a manager's direction, estimate, priority,
or technical decision.

**Preflight:** Identify the decision owner, the user's duty to advise or comply,
material risk, evidence, audience, deadline, and whether a protected or formal
concern exists.

**Sequence:**

1. state the shared outcome;
2. present the specific concern and evidence;
3. compare options against agreed criteria;
4. ask for the decision;
5. record the decision, owner, assumptions, and warning point.

**Starter:**

> I want to support the Friday decision. The current option creates ___ risk
> because ___. I see two workable paths: A preserves ___; B preserves ___. My
> recommendation is A. Can you confirm which path you want me to execute?

**Branches:** If heard, confirm the decision. If the manager disagrees,
separate a legitimate directive from a mandatory concern. If ambiguous, ask
who decides. If ignored, use the technical or organizational route tied to the
risk.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not keep relitigating an ordinary decision after the
authorized owner decides. Escalate when a mandatory safety, legal, ethical, or
governance obligation remains unresolved.

## 2. Workload and incompatible priorities

**Trigger:** A new request conflicts with existing commitments.

**Preflight:** Confirm current owners, deadlines, capacity, definition of done,
and the concrete cost of accepting the new work.

**Sequence:**

1. show current commitments without a defensive history;
2. offer two feasible delivery packages;
3. state what each package displaces;
4. ask the decision owner to prioritize;
5. document the choice.

**Starter:**

> I can deliver A by Thursday, or move to B today and shift A to Monday. Doing
> both by Thursday would remove the verification step. Which outcome should
> take priority?

**Branches:** If told “both,” restate the resource or assurance required. If no
decision arrives, continue the last authorized priority when safe and state
the assumption. If the pattern repeats, request a standing intake rule.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not silently absorb undefined work. Escalate when
capacity creates a material safety, quality, or contractual risk.

## 3. Feedback and accountability

**Trigger:** A specific behavior or recurring miss needs to change.

**Preflight:** Check recency, evidence, controllability, authority, required
standard, timing, support, and whether public delivery would create avoidable
status threat.

**Sequence:**

1. name the behavior and impact;
2. ask for material context;
3. state the standard;
4. agree support, action, owner, date, and warning point;
5. schedule a review and recap neutrally.

**Starter:**

> The last three reports contained calculation errors in the totals, which
> delayed the release check. What is contributing to that pattern? The
> required standard is a verified total before handoff. Let's agree on the
> check, support, and review date.

**Branches:** If context reveals unclear instruction or missing resources,
repair the system. If the expectation is disputed, clarify authority. If the
commitment fails, use the pre-agreed accountability route.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not demand a character admission. Move to a qualified
performance process when repeated evidence and prior support justify it.

## 4. Credit and attribution correction

**Trigger:** The user's work was omitted, blurred, or attributed inaccurately.

**Preflight:** Preserve ordinary evidence, identify all legitimate
contributors, the public record needing correction, the decision audience, and
retaliation exposure.

**Sequence:**

1. correct privately when safe;
2. describe contributions factually;
3. request a concrete record update;
4. agree the future presentation and attribution norm;
5. surface the record through the appropriate owner if correction is refused
   or the pattern repeats.

**Starter:**

> In today's meeting, the model was described as your analysis. I built the
> model, and you incorporated it into the deck. Please update the recap to name
> both contributions. Before the next meeting, let's agree who presents each
> section and how authorship is shown.

**Branches:** If corrected, confirm the record. If intent is debated, return to
the factual contribution. If other contributions were missed, correct them
too.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not launch a mass accusation. Escalate the documented
pattern, not a diagnosis of motive.

## 5. Exclusion from access or a decision forum

**Trigger:** Decisions affecting the user occur in a forum they cannot access.

**Preflight:** Map the decision owner, forum, information path, invitation
owner, affected parties, proxy representation, and whether access or merely
input/visibility is required.

**Sequence:**

1. identify the decision that requires the user's input;
2. ask for the smallest useful access change;
3. offer alternatives: agenda slot, written input, proxy, record, or review;
4. state the impact of continued omission;
5. observe whether access and decisions actually change.

**Starter:**

> This forum is deciding ___, and my team owns ___ information needed for that
> decision. Please add me for that agenda item. If full attendance is not
> workable, I need the decision question in advance and the record afterward
> so I can provide and verify our input.

**Branches:** If the meeting is the wrong forum, ask for the real pathway. If
exclusion is accidental, fix the process. If repeated, involve the decision or
governance owner with the factual impact.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not build a secret influence score. Escalate when a
required voice, assurance, or affected party remains structurally absent.

## 6. Sponsorship, networking, and advocacy

**Trigger:** The user wants access, an introduction, visibility, mentoring, or
promotion advocacy.

**Preflight:** Check relationship evidence, relevance, request size, decision
timing, the person's discretion, reciprocity expectations, and a meaningful
ability to decline.

**Sequence:**

1. make a bounded, purpose-specific request;
2. ask for readiness feedback or missing evidence;
3. deliver and follow through;
4. request exposure or an introduction;
5. request advocacy only after the person can support it honestly.

**Starter:**

> You have seen my work on A and B. I am preparing for roles that require C.
> Would you be willing to tell me what evidence is still missing? If the fit is
> there later, I may ask whether you can introduce me to the program lead.

**Branches:** If the person cannot help, ask for direction rather than
pressure. If feedback identifies a gap, create evidence. If advocacy is
declined, preserve the relationship without repeated asks.

**Stop/escalate:** No flattery debt, manufactured intimacy, or implied
obligation. Stop after a clear decline.

## 7. Conflict de-escalation and re-entry

**Trigger:** A disagreement has become repetitive, personal, or too heated to
solve in the current format.

**Preflight:** Classify task, process, role, relationship, values, and safety
elements; assess heat, audience, power, and ongoing dependency.

**Sequence:**

1. pause with a specific re-entry plan;
2. separate issue lanes;
3. choose one decision question and criteria;
4. assign owner, participants, timebox, and record;
5. re-enter by summarizing both unresolved positions;
6. mediate, escalate, or disengage if the process fails.

**Starter:**

> We have moved from the design question into claims about each other's
> competence. Let's pause. Tomorrow we will compare the options against the
> approved criteria, with ___ deciding, and record the open risks.

**Branches:** If both parties can re-enter, proceed. If one refuses structure,
use authority or mediation. If threat or retaliation appears, move to
protection and a qualified route.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not force dialogue in an unsafe case or run repeated
unstructured “clear the air” sessions.

## 8. Trust repair after a concrete breach

**Trigger:** A missed commitment, disclosure, concealment, or other verified
breach damaged reliance.

**Preflight:** Separate accepted facts, disputed claims, impact, repairability,
recurrence, authority, and what proof would matter.

**Sequence:**

1. acknowledge the behavior and impact;
2. accept supported responsibility;
3. remedy the loss;
4. change the process;
5. define an observable proof window;
6. leave reconciliation voluntary.

**Starter:**

> I sent the wrong file and exposed the draft. That was my error. I have
> withdrawn access, notified the authorized owner, and will use the two-person
> release check from now on. I understand that confidence will depend on what
> I do next, not this message.

**Branches:** If intent is disputed, do not confess unsupported motive. If the
breach is not repairable privately, involve the correct authority. If the
other party wants distance, respect it.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not use an apology to secure forgiveness, silence, or a
waiver of a legitimate process.

## 9. Refusal and recurring boundary enforcement

**Trigger:** A request exceeds capacity, consent, privacy, money, time, or an
agreed relationship boundary.

**Preflight:** Identify what the user controls, the truthful reason disclosure
actually needed, a feasible alternative, power exposure, and the consequence
the user can carry out.

**Sequence:**

1. state the decision clearly;
2. give minimal useful context;
3. offer an alternative only if genuine;
4. state what the user will do if the request repeats;
5. execute the consequence consistently.

**Starter:**

> I can't lend more money while the previous loan is unpaid. I won't discuss a
> new loan. We can agree on a repayment plan for the existing amount.

**Branches:** If accepted, move on. If challenged, repeat once without adding
new justifications. If pressured, end the exchange or use support.

**Stop/escalate:** A boundary is not an opening bid. Stop responding after the
selected repeat branch; escalate coercion or threat.

## 10. Asking for help, access, or a favor

**Trigger:** The user needs assistance without creating vague obligation.

**Preflight:** Define purpose, exact effort, timing, why this person is
relevant, alternatives, reciprocity boundaries, and a real decline path.

**Sequence:**

1. state the purpose;
2. make a bounded request;
3. give effort and timing;
4. make declining easy;
5. close the loop and credit the contribution.

**Starter:**

> Could you review the two-page risk summary for technical gaps by Thursday?
> I expect it to take about 20 minutes. If this week is full, no problem—I can
> ask the assurance team.

**Branches:** If they accept, confirm scope. If they counteroffer, decide
explicitly. If they decline, thank them and use the alternative.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not convert past help, status, or friendship into a debt.

## 11. Difficult friend, family, roommate, or neighbor pattern

**Trigger:** A repeated non-romantic everyday behavior affects shared money,
space, time, commitments, privacy, or respect.

**Preflight:** Identify the specific pattern, shared agreement, practical
impact, user's control, dependency, housing/financial exposure, and safety.

**Sequence:**

1. describe the repeated behavior;
2. name the practical impact;
3. request one change;
4. set an observation window;
5. state the user-controlled consequence;
6. renegotiate the arrangement or reduce dependency if the pattern continues.

**Starter:**

> The kitchen was left uncleaned after the last three agreed turns. I need it
> completed by Sunday evening each week. If that does not happen over the next
> two weeks, I will stop sharing the cleaning arrangement and we will need a
> different allocation.

**Branches:** If the agreement was unclear, clarify it. If there is a real
constraint, negotiate. If the pattern continues, implement the consequence.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not diagnose, parent, or shame the other person. Put
threat or housing/legal questions on the qualified route.

## 12. Formal-route preparation without adjudication

**Trigger:** The user may need HR, union, ombuds, regulator, legal, safeguarding,
or another qualified process.

**Preflight:** Check immediate safety, jurisdiction, current policy, deadlines,
retaliation exposure, records, desired interim protection, and whether the
skill has current authoritative information.

**Sequence:**

1. build a minimal chronology;
2. separate records, observations, reports, interpretations, and unknowns;
3. list impact, witnesses, prior attempts, and preservation status;
4. define questions and desired process outcomes;
5. verify the current qualified route;
6. avoid declaring the formal category.

**Starter:**

> I want guidance on the applicable process. I have organized the dates,
> behaviors, records, witnesses, impact, and prior attempts. My immediate
> questions are ___, and the interim protection I am requesting is ___.

**Branches:** If urgent protection is needed, prioritize it. If the route lacks
authority, find the correct one. If a deadline may apply, verify it promptly
with an authoritative source or professional.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not investigate covertly, coach testimony, or state a
legal/HR conclusion.

## 13. Digital or text ambiguity

**Trigger:** Tone, delay, punctuation, read receipts, short replies, or channel
behavior is being treated as a social verdict.

**Preflight:** Check message specificity, normal response pattern, urgency,
time zone, channel norms, missing context, repetition, and material decisions.

**Sequence:**

1. classify the cue as weak or stronger evidence;
2. identify the decision hidden behind the tone question;
3. ask one content-focused clarification or move to a richer channel;
4. agree response-time and channel norms if the problem recurs;
5. update from follow-through, not punctuation alone.

**Starter:**

> I may be reading too much into the short reply. I need the decision rather
> than a tone interpretation: should I proceed with A, or hold until we speak
> tomorrow?

**Branches:** If clarified, stop analyzing tone. If avoidance repeats around
decisions, address the pattern. If the matter is sensitive, use a bounded call
and written recap.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not run repeated message tests, monitor activity, or
infer hostility from latency alone.

## 14. Cross-cultural or mixed-language mismatch

**Trigger:** Directness, silence, disagreement, hierarchy, face, language
fluency, or channel choice may be affecting the interaction.

**Preflight:** Ask about individual history, roles, public/private setting,
team norms, language proficiency, channel, and desired function. Do not begin
with a country score.

**Sequence:**

1. describe the observed mismatch without national explanation;
2. state the communication function required;
3. offer two participation or wording formats;
4. ask the person's preference;
5. confirm shared meaning, decision right, boundary, and deadline.

**Starter:**

> In the meeting I did not hear a clear agree/disagree response. For this
> decision, I need any technical concern before Friday. Would you prefer to
> discuss it privately or send written comments?

**Branches:** If language is the barrier, simplify and confirm meaning. If
hierarchy affects the forum, create a safer route. If the pattern is personal,
update from that evidence rather than a cultural label.

**Stop/escalate:** No national personality inference, compulsory indirectness,
or “cultural” excuse for a documented boundary or safety breach.

## 15. Multi-actor stakeholder and coalition dynamics

**Trigger:** More than two actors influence information, authority, access,
implementation, or reputational exposure.

**Preflight:** Map decision owner, forum, information holder, implementer,
affected party, verifier, sponsor/mediator, missing voice, and sensitive
judgments.

**Sequence:**

1. bound the decision;
2. map roles and dependencies using evidence and confidence;
3. identify missing or proxy voices;
4. choose who to inform, consult, involve, collaborate with, protect, or
   escalate to;
5. define what each actor can change;
6. close the feedback loop and update the map.

**Starter:**

> Before choosing a message, we need the decision path. ___ owns the decision,
> ___ holds the required information, ___ carries the implementation impact,
> and ___ can verify the record. The smallest useful move is to bring ___ into
> ___ before the decision date.

**Branches:** If ownership is unclear, clarify governance first. If a proxy
cannot represent an affected group, create direct input. If sensitive
influence judgments would cause harm, keep them private and action-focused.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not manufacture alliances, triangulate gossip, publish
opposition scores, or treat low power as low importance.

## 16. Rebalancing low-promotability or invisible work

**Trigger:** The user repeatedly receives coordination, note-taking,
administrative cleanup, emotional labor, or other necessary work with limited
visibility or development value.

**Preflight:** Inventory frequency, time, opportunity cost, allocation method,
beneficiaries, and whether the pattern appears linked to role or status. Do not
assume every helping task is exploitative.

**Sequence:**

1. make the work and displaced cost visible;
2. propose rotation, scope reduction, explicit recognition, or paired
   developmental ownership;
3. ask the allocation decision-maker for a rule;
4. confirm the next allocation and review window.

**Starter:**

> I have handled the notes and follow-up for six of the last seven meetings.
> I can cover this week, but after that I would like us to rotate the role or
> connect it to ownership of the workstream. Can we agree the allocation rule?

**Branches:** If accepted, record the rotation. If framed as “just helping,”
state the accumulated time and displaced deliverable. If retaliation or a
protected-status pattern may matter, lower exposure and seek qualified advice
without making a formal finding.

**Stop/escalate:** Escalate when the pattern persists after a clear allocation
request or materially blocks the user's core work and development.

## 17. Receiving vague or poorly delivered feedback

**Trigger:** The user receives labels such as “not strategic,” “not a leader,”
or “poor executive presence,” or useful criticism delivered publicly or
harshly.

**Preflight:** Separate feedback substance, evidence, standard, delivery,
formal consequence, and the user's capacity to respond in the moment.

**Sequence:**

1. regulate or pause before rebutting;
2. reflect the core concern without accepting an undefined label;
3. request one recent example and the expected alternative;
4. agree one observable test and review date;
5. address inappropriate delivery separately from substance.

**Starter:**

> I hear that the update did not support the decision as you expected. Could
> you point to one section and describe what a stronger version would have
> done? I would like to test that standard in the next review.

**Branches:** If an example appears, compile the standard. If the criticism was
public, defer the process concern to a private follow-up. If examples and
standards remain unavailable while consequences grow, request written
clarification and consider an appropriate qualified route.

**Stop/escalate:** End a threatening or degrading exchange. Do not dismiss
substance solely because delivery was poor, and do not accept an identity
judgment as an actionable performance standard.

## 18. Evaluating an apology and bounded trust re-entry

**Trigger:** Another person apologizes after a concrete breach and the user
must decide whether or how to rely on them again.

**Preflight:** Identify the verified behavior, affected trust dimension,
severity, recurrence, dependency, remedy, and exposure created by re-entry.

**Sequence:**

1. separate apology quality from evidence of change;
2. check acknowledgment, supported responsibility, impact, remedy, and
   prevention;
3. design a small, reversible re-entry trial;
4. specify verification behavior and review date;
5. preserve the user's right not to restore prior access.

**Starter:**

> I appreciate the acknowledgment. Before returning to the previous
> arrangement, I would like the next two submissions reviewed jointly with the
> approval recorded. We can reassess after that.

**Branches:** A good apology plus action supports a bounded trial. Eloquence
without remedy supports caution. Denial of disputed intent does not erase
responsibility for verified impact, but responsibility should not be invented.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not demand forgiveness or treat apology as proof. Stop
re-entry when verification fails or the exposure is disproportionate.

## 19. Resetting a missed commitment

**Trigger:** An agreed deliverable, reply, payment, decision, or action did not
occur.

**Preflight:** Retrieve the actual agreement, owner, due date, dependencies,
warning point, prior misses, and authority to change control or ownership.

**Sequence:**

1. state the prior agreement and observed miss;
2. ask what changed;
3. recompile owner, deliverable, date, dependency, and warning point;
4. state the operational fallback if it misses again;
5. verify completion rather than accepting “soon.”

**Starter:**

> We agreed the revised schedule would be issued Monday, and it has not been
> received. What changed? I need either the schedule by 3 p.m. tomorrow or
> confirmation that we should reassign the task.

**Branches:** A credible constraint may justify a new plan. Vagueness calls for
one bounded clarification. Repeated misses call for a different control,
reassignment, escalation, or reduced dependency.

**Stop/escalate:** After repeated resets without delivery, stop generating new
informal promises. Do not add shame or make consequences the user cannot apply.

## 20. Closing a meeting with executable commitments

**Trigger:** A conversation produced apparent alignment but risks different
interpretations or incomplete follow-through.

**Preflight:** Identify decision authority, unresolved items, absent owners,
dependencies, record sensitivity, and a reasonable correction window.

**Sequence:**

1. state the decision and unresolved issues separately;
2. compile owner, deliverable, date, dependency, and decision authority;
3. name the next review or warning point;
4. circulate a neutral recap;
5. invite factual corrections within a defined window.

**Starter:**

> To confirm: Mei owns the revised interface matrix by August 7; Alex provides
> the source list by August 4; the design authority decides the open exception
> on August 9. Please correct anything I have misstated by noon tomorrow.

**Branches:** If someone disputes the recap, isolate the exact term. If an
owner was absent, seek their confirmation rather than assigning by silence. If
authority is unclear, mark the item unresolved.

**Stop/escalate:** Do not record consensus where disagreement remains or treat
silence as enthusiastic consent. Reopen only the disputed term, not the whole
meeting.

## Playbook quality check

Before returning an answer, verify:

1. the playbook matches the leading mechanism;
2. the recommendation remains useful if the motive hypothesis is wrong;
3. power and structural conditions are not reduced to tone;
4. the wording contains one visible decision, request, or boundary;
5. branches are plausible rather than exhaustive;
6. the observation window and stop rule are executable;
7. the user retains agency and the other person retains meaningful choice
   where choice is legitimate;
8. current formal or safety claims are verified through an authoritative route.
