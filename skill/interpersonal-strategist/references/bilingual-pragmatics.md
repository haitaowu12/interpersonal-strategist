# Bilingual Workplace Pragmatics

Use this reference for English-Simplified Chinese adaptation, mixed-language
workplace cases, or channel choices involving email, chat, WeChat, or meetings.

## Preserve function, not sentence shape

Draft each language for the same interpersonal function. Do not translate word
by word and then assume the strategic force survived.

Preserve:

- facts and source limits;
- uncertainty;
- the recommendation;
- obligation and decision rights;
- meaningful ability to decline;
- boundary and consequence;
- escalation and stopping conditions;
- user agency.

Permitted variation:

- address terms;
- sentence order;
- amount of context;
- relational buffering;
- directness;
- channel recommendation.

Variation must not weaken or intensify the substantive request without the
user's informed choice.

## Calibrate with variables, not stereotypes

Consider:

- roles and formal authority;
- relationship history;
- public versus private setting;
- urgency;
- need for a record;
- shared language proficiency;
- local team norms;
- individual communication history.

Treat hierarchy, face, and indirectness as questions about the local situation,
not national facts about a person.

## Use a parity check

For both language versions, verify:

1. same facts;
2. same unresolved unknowns;
3. same primary recommendation;
4. same owner and deadline;
5. same ability to refuse;
6. same boundary;
7. same escalation trigger;
8. same stopping condition.

Any material drift is a hard failure.

## Chinese drafting guidance

Prefer natural, restrained, specific wording. Use relational framing where it
helps reception, but keep the decision request visible.

Weakening failure:

> 如果方便的话，也许可以考虑一下……

when the intended function is a clear decision request with a deadline.

Better pattern:

> 为了按时完成，我需要今天确认优先级：是先完成 A，还是把资源转到 B？
> 两项同时完成在现有时间内不可行。

The relational tone remains professional while the trade-off and decision right
stay explicit.

## English drafting guidance

Use plain, behavior-specific language. Avoid therapy jargon, pseudo-legal
claims, and corporate filler.

Escalation in English should not become more accusatory merely because direct
wording is common in the local environment.

## Channel patterns

- Use chat or WeChat for short coordination and narrow clarification.
- Use email or another durable channel when ownership, deadlines, attribution,
  or a formal record matters.
- Use a meeting for negotiation or repair, then confirm the result in writing.
- Do not place sensitive third-party information into an AI draft or group
  channel unless necessary and authorized.

## Review requirement

For consequential bilingual messages, recommend human review by a fluent
speaker familiar with the relationship and local workplace context. Synthetic
parity checks cannot establish naturalness or status calibration.
