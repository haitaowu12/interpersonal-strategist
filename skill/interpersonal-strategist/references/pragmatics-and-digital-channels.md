# Pragmatics and Digital Channels

Use this reference when meaning depends on shared context, conversational
repair, email or chat tone, response timing, channel choice, or AI-assisted
wording.

## Treat understanding as coordinated work

A message being sent does not prove that the same meaning was received.
Communication depends on enough common ground for the present purpose.

Before inferring motive, check:

- what each person could reasonably know;
- whether the referent, decision, standard, or deadline was explicit;
- whether the receiver showed understanding through a relevant next action,
  acknowledgment, paraphrase, or correction;
- whether an apparent disagreement is actually a different definition,
  timeline, audience, or decision rule;
- which smallest repair would restore usable shared understanding.

Useful repairs:

- “When you say ___, do you mean ___ or ___?”
- “Let me check that I understood the decision: ___.”
- “I think we are using ___ differently. My working definition is ___.”
- “Which part of my summary is inaccurate?”

Do not demand exhaustive mutual understanding. Ground only what is needed for
the decision or relationship function.

## Separate conveyance from convergence

**Conveyance** lets people receive, inspect, and process information. It often
benefits from asynchronous text, records, diagrams, or material sent in
advance.

**Convergence** develops shared meaning, resolves ambiguity, negotiates, or
repairs. It often benefits from timely back-and-forth.

Many material cases need a hybrid:

1. send the facts, agenda, or options in writing;
2. hold a bounded conversation for questions and convergence;
3. confirm decisions, owners, dates, and unresolved items in writing.

Do not move automatically to a call. Stay asynchronous when a live exchange
would increase coercion, accessibility barriers, language disadvantage,
safety risk, or loss of an important record.

## Read digital signals conservatively

Short wording, punctuation, delayed replies, read receipts, missing emoji, and
channel changes can carry relational meaning, but they are noisy and
context-dependent.

Use this sequence:

1. quote the exact cue;
2. compare it with the person's established local norm only if the user
   actually knows that norm;
3. generate mundane, structural, and relational explanations;
4. identify the operational decision;
5. ask one low-cost question or state a time-bounded operating assumption.

Do not convert silence, latency, formality, or a terse message into a rejection,
hostility, dishonesty, or status verdict.

Text also encourages tone projection: senders and receivers can be more
confident than warranted that intended tone is obvious. For emotionally loaded
messages, remove sarcasm, ambiguous humor, mind-reading, and rhetorical
questions. Make the purpose and request explicit.

## Switch channels by failure mode

Move from text toward a bounded live conversation when:

- the exchange is lengthening without reducing ambiguity;
- each reply introduces a new interpretation;
- several issues must be traded together;
- repair depends on interaction rather than record alone.

Move toward written form when:

- ownership, dates, commitments, or evidence matter;
- a live conversation produces pressure or memory disputes;
- accessibility or language processing benefits from time;
- the user needs a neutral recap after a conversation.

Name the reason and agenda:

> We are interpreting the sequence differently, and more email is not resolving
> it. Could we use 20 minutes tomorrow for the three disputed steps? I will
> confirm the decisions and open items afterward.

## Use AI-assisted wording without losing authorship

AI can change tone, speed, and how a message is perceived. Treat generated
language as a draft under the user's responsibility, not as a substitute
speaker.

Before sending, run the voice-and-authenticity check:

1. **Truth:** Are every fact, commitment, authority claim, and feeling accurate?
2. **Voice:** Would the user actually say this? Remove inflated warmth,
   polished filler, therapy language, and unfamiliar idiom.
3. **Force:** Did the draft become more coercive, submissive, certain, or
   accusatory than intended?
4. **Specificity:** Is there one real request, decision, boundary, or next step?
5. **Disclosure:** Would suspected automation create a material trust problem
   in this context? If so, rewrite substantially or disclose assistance when
   norms, stakes, or policy make that appropriate.
6. **Ownership:** Can the user defend and follow through on every sentence?

Never fabricate firsthand knowledge, emotional experience, consensus, or a
promise merely to make the prose sound natural.

## Response branches

- **Positive:** confirm the actual understanding and commitment.
- **Ambiguous:** ask one forced-choice or bounded clarification.
- **Negative:** separate disagreement about substance from dissatisfaction
  with channel or tone.
- **No response:** use one deadline-supported follow-up or a stated operating
  assumption, then apply the stop rule.

The success criterion is not a warm reply. It is enough shared understanding
to make the next decision safely and honestly.
